1. AlInGaP vs. Monolithic InGaN Red Micro-LEDs & 298 K RF-Cavity Teleportation

A. Thermodynamic & Efficiency Droop: AlInGaP vs. Pure InGaN Red Micro-LEDs

In the modified ABC recombination model, internal quantum efficiency is governed by:

$$IQE(n) = \frac{B n^2}{A n + B n^2 + C n^3 + J_{\text{leak}} / (q d)}$$

where \(A\) is Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) defect/sidewall recombination, \(B\) is radiative recombination, \(C\) is Auger recombination, and \(J_{\text{leak}}\) is thermionic carrier escape across the heterostructure barrier.

Primary Failure Mode Sidewall SRH at micro-pitch + thermal leakage at high \(T_j\). The "Red Wall" (QCSE + Auger + Strain). B. RF-Cavity Entanglement Teleportation Addressing at \(T = 298\,\text{K}\): Bounds & Physical Reality Check

Replacing active-matrix electrical traces with quantum teleportation encounters two fatal, opposing physical bounds that cannot close at room temperature:

1. Causal Lower Bound on Frame Refresh Latency (No-Communication Theorem)

Quantum teleportation cannot transmit frame state faster than light. Under the No-Communication Theorem, the reduced density matrix at pixel \(i\) (\(\rho_B = \text{Tr}_A(\rho_{AB})\)) is completely independent of local measurements at the display controller until 2 classical bits per teleported qubit arrive over a classical channel to select the pixel's Pauli correction (\(I, X, Z, XZ\)). Across a panel of maximum routing span \(d_{\max}\):

$$T_{\text{frame,min}} = \tau_{\text{BSM}} + \frac{d_{\max}}{c_{\text{eff}}} + \tau_{\text{gate}} + \tau_{\text{rad}}, \qquad \tau_{\text{cl}} \ge \frac{1\,\text{m}}{c} \approx 3.34\,\text{ns}$$

where \(c_{\text{eff}} = c / n_{\text{eff}}\) (plus classical multiplexing and serialization delay for \(N_{\text{subpixels}} \times 2b\) classical bits, making teleportation strictly higher in classical bandwidth overhead than sending \(b\) classical RGB bits directly over an optical waveguide or copper trace).

2. Ambient 298 K Decoherence Upper Bound (Thermal Photon & Phonon Bombardment)

For teleportation to deliver a coherent state before the classical correction bits arrive, the total protocol time must satisfy \(T_{\text{refresh,max}} \ll T_2(298\,\text{K})\). At ambient \(T = 298\,\text{K}\): thermal energy \(k_B T = 25.68\,\text{meV}\), thermal frequency \(\nu_{\text{th}} = k_B T / h = 6.21\,\text{THz}\), and fundamental thermal correlation time \(\tau_{\text{th}} = \hbar / (k_B T) = 25.63\,\text{fs}\).

Even for a high-\(Q\) room-temperature cavity (\(Q = 10^4\) at 5 GHz, bare decay \(1/\kappa \approx 318\,\text{ns}\)), thermal stimulation collapses cavity coherence to:
    $$T_{2,\text{cav}}(298\,\text{K}) \approx \frac{1}{\kappa(2\bar{n}_{\text{th}} + 1)} \approx 128.2\,\text{ps}$$
  • InGaN Lattice Phonon Bombardment: In a solid-state InGaN quantum well at 298 K, GaN longitudinal optical (LO) phonons (\(\hbar\omega_{\text{LO}} \approx 92\,\text{meV}\), \(\bar{n}_{\text{LO}} \approx 0.0286\)) plus dense acoustic deformation/piezoelectric phonon scattering broaden homogeneous exciton linewidths to \(\Gamma_{\text{ph}} \sim 20\text{-}40\,\text{meV}\), locking electronic/excitonic coherence to:

2. Speculative Inference Tensor Memory on a Shared 75 GB/s Unified APU Bus

A. Bus Saturation Comparison Across the Three Architectures

On an AMD Ryzen 7 6800U (Zen 3+ CPU + RDNA 2 Radeon 680M iGPU sharing one LPDDR5-6400 memory controller, sustained effective bandwidth \(B_{\text{eff}} \approx 75\,\text{GB/s}\)), batch-1 autoregressive decode is deeply memory-bandwidth bound under a strict zero-sum bus constraint:

$$B_{\text{CPU}}(t) + B_{\text{iGPU}}(t) \le B_{\text{eff}} \approx 75\,\text{GB/s}$$
  • 3-Tier Pyramid Speculative Decoding (Drafter D -> QA Qualifier Q -> Target T): Sequential execution avoids concurrent CPU/iGPU bus contention (\(\phi_{\text{bus}} \approx 1\)), but streams three distinct weight tensors across LPDDR5 per macro-cycle (\(\gamma_1 W_D + W_Q + W_T\)) plus three KV caches. Only wins when \(W_D \ll W_Q \ll W_T\) and \(Q\) prunes frequent low-\(\alpha\) rejections before waking \(W_T\); if \(W_Q\) is a 1.5B-3B Q4 model (1-2 GB, costing 13-27 ms at 75 GB/s), paying \(W_Q\) unconditionally on high-acceptance passages underperforms a lean 2-tier pipeline.
  • Parallel Block-Diffusion (DFlash at \(\gamma = 16\)) with Layer-Wise KV Injection: Slashes drafter weight reads by generating all 16 draft tokens in \(K_{\text{diff}} = 1\text{-}4\) passes (\(K_{\text{diff}} W_D + M_{\text{inj}}\)). However, verifying \(\gamma + 1 = 17\) tokens in parallel expands attention activation tensors (17 x d_model per layer) against the Radeon 680M's small L2 cache, risking spills into LPDDR5, and because target verification remains strictly prefix-causal, a mismatch at position \(k = 2\) throws away tokens \(3 \dots 16\).
  • Heterogeneous CPU-GPU Asynchronous Pipelining (Mirror-SD): Built for disaggregated memory (500-1000 GB/s discrete VRAM + separate host DDR5). On a unified 6800U APU, concurrent CPU drafting steals memory channels directly from the iGPU (slowing the iGPU by \(1/(1-\lambda)\)) and triggers Infinity Fabric snoop stalls (\(\phi_{\text{bus}} \gt 1\)). Beats sequential runs on a 6800U only when the drafter fits 100% inside the 16 MB Zen 3+ L3 cache or when large-\(\gamma\) iGPU verification becomes temporarily compute-bound across the 12 RDNA 2 CUs.

Let \(W_T\) be Target weights, \(S_{\text{KV}}(L) = 4 n_{\text{layers}} n_{\text{kv}} d_{\text{head}} L\) the FP16 KV footprint at sequence length \(L\), \(\rho = 16 / b_{\text{eff}}\) the effective 4-bit KV compression ratio (\(b_{\text{eff}} \approx 4.2\text{-}4.8\,\text{bits} \Rightarrow \rho \approx 3.33\text{-}3.81\)), \(\kappa_{\text{deq}} S_{\text{KV}}(L)\) extra LPDDR5 spill traffic from unfused dequantization (\(\kappa_{\text{deq}} = 0\) for fused in-register kernels; \(\kappa_{\text{deq}} = 2.0\) for write-back/re-read FP16 spills), \(M_{\text{ALU}} = B_{\text{eff}} t_{\text{deq,ALU}}\) the byte-equivalent rotational projection stall, and \(\bar{\tau}(\gamma, \alpha) = (1 - \alpha^{\gamma+1})/(1 - \alpha)\) the expected accepted tokens per cycle.

Step 1: Isolated 4-Bit KV Dequantization Negation Threshold
$$\kappa_{\text{deq}} S_{\text{KV}}(L) + B_{\text{eff}} t_{\text{deq,ALU}} \ge \left(1 - \frac{1}{\rho}\right) S_{\text{KV}}(L) \iff \kappa_{\text{deq}} + \frac{B_{\text{eff}} t_{\text{deq,ALU}}}{S_{\text{KV}}(L)} \ge 1 - \frac{1}{\rho}$$

Any L2 spill above \(\kappa_{\text{deq}} \ge 0.719\) destroys 100% of quantization gains before counting ALU stalls.

3. Differentiable Wave Mechanics, Acoustic Phase & RVQ Sub-Bass Constraints

A. 4.5 ms Haas Shift + 4.0 Hz Sub-Bass Detuning: Exact Mono-Sum Derivation & Crucial Caveat

1. Why a Pure 4.5 ms Haas Shift Alone Does Cause Destructive Mono Cancellation

For a coherent stereo pair \(L(t) = x(t)\) and \(R(t) = x(t - \Delta t)\) at \(\Delta t = 4.5\,\text{ms}\), \(4.5\,\text{ms}\) sits squarely inside the precedence (Haas) fusion window (1-30 ms), well beyond the human head's maximum ITD (\(\approx 0.65\text{-}0.75\,\text{ms}\)), fusing into a widened stereo image. Summing to mono \(M(t) = \frac{1}{2}[x(t) + x(t - \Delta t)]\), however, creates a steep comb filter:

$$|H_{\text{mono}}(f)| = \left|\frac{1 + e^{-j 2\pi f \Delta t}}{2}\right| = |\cos(\pi f \Delta t)|, \qquad f_{\text{null},k} = \frac{2k + 1}{2\Delta t}$$
  • Exact total cancellation nulls: \(k = 0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{111.11\,\text{Hz}}\) (-inf dB right inside the 20-180 Hz bass region!), \(k = 1 \Rightarrow 333.33\,\text{Hz}\), \(k = 2 \Rightarrow 555.56\,\text{Hz}\).
  • Sub-bass comb loss profile: 20 Hz -> -0.35 dB, 40 Hz -> -1.47 dB, 60 Hz -> -3.59 dB, 80 Hz -> -7.42 dB, 111.11 Hz -> total null, 180 Hz -> -1.65 dB.
2. Coupling With \(|f_1 - f_2| = 4.0\,\text{Hz}\) Detuning: Static Null Elimination vs. Instantaneous Beating

Setting \(L(t) = A\cos(2\pi f_1 t)\) and \(R(t) = A\cos\big(2\pi(f_1 + \Delta f)(t - \Delta t)\big)\) with \(\Delta f = 4.0\,\text{Hz}\) yields:

$$M(t) = \frac{L(t) + R(t)}{2} = A \cos\!\Big(\pi \Delta f \cdot t - \pi(f_1 + \Delta f)\Delta t\Big) \cos\!\Big(2\pi\!\left(f_1 + \frac{\Delta f}{2}\right)t - \pi(f_1 + \Delta f)\Delta t\Big)$$
  • Why permanent / static spectral cancellation vanishes (Time-Averaged RMS Conservation): Because \(\Delta\phi(t) = 2\pi\Delta f \cdot t - \phi_0\) rotates smoothly through \([0, 2\pi)\) at 4.0 Hz, \(\langle L(t) R(t) \rangle_T = 0\) over any window \(T \ge 1/\Delta f = 250\,\text{ms}\), locking average mono power strictly to \(P_{\text{mono}} = A^2 / 4 = (P_L + P_R)/4\) (constant -3 dB, zero stationary comb notches for every \(f_1\) and \(\Delta t\)).
  • Real-World Engineering Caveat (Instantaneous 4 Hz Tremolo / Dropouts): Equal-amplitude L/R detuning at \(\Delta f = 4.0\,\text{Hz}\) does not stop instantaneous cancellation: the slow envelope \(\cos(\pi\Delta f \cdot t - \phi_0)\) still crosses zero (100% momentary cancellation every 250 ms), and un-detuned upper bass near 111.11 Hz still gets notched by the static 4.5 ms delay. Mono-safe DiT architecture: lock the fundamental sub-bass carrier (\(\lt 80\text{-}100\,\text{Hz}\)) on Mid center amplitude \(a_M\), and layer the 4.5 ms + 4 Hz detuned pair as a lower-amplitude Side/harmonic layer (\(a_S \lt a_M\)) so minimum mono envelope stays strictly bounded above zero: \(E_{\min} = a_M - a_S \gt 0\).
